Data on Energy, Water, and E-waste in Artificial Intelligence
Artificial intelligence carries a hidden environmental cost.
First, energy. According to the International Energy Agency, data center electricity consumption in 2024 stands at
approximately 415 terawatt-hours (TWh). By 2030, this figure could reach about 945 TWh—nearly double the current level.
Second, water. A report from the United Nations University estimates that by 2030, the water footprint of data centers—driven
by their electricity consumption—could reach 9.3 trillion liters annually. This is equivalent to the basic annual water needs 
for the 1.3 billion people living in sub-Saharan Africa.Finally, e-waste. As obsolete servers and computer hardware are 
phased out, AI infrastructure could generate as much as 2.5 million metric tons of e-waste annually by 2030.These figures 
demonstrate that the environmental impact of artificial intelligence extends beyond electricity consumption; we must also 
account for its water usage and the resulting e-waste.
